## Alerts (what they reference / who they target / lifecycle language found — trigger/escalation policy itself is item 7)

Every item below is **PROPOSED** (vendor-voice "the system should...") unless noted otherwise — none of this is described as existing practice, consistent with the 0-client caveat above.

- Medication alerts (Q10): triggered by admin >1hr early/late, missed dose, refusal, wrong medication/client/dose/route. Targets: Team Lead/Supervisor, Program Manager, Director of Operations, Executive Director. "cannot be overridden, hidden, dismissed, or removed by staff." No explicit "resolved/acknowledged" state described — only "reviewed."
- Sharp/medication count discrepancy alerts (Q14): same 4-role target list, "for significant safety concerns" adds Executive Director explicitly (implying lower-severity discrepancies may not always escalate that high — a severity-tiering signal worth carrying into item 7's alert matrix). Also generates a **"Staff-Related Incident Report"** (Q14's exact term) — note the naming variance against glossary's canonical "Staff Incident Report" (still-not-provided template, per OQ-08). Possibly the same document under two names, or a genuinely narrower discrepancy-specific record — flag for whoever eventually resolves OQ-08, not decided here.
- Incentive negative-balance alert (Q11): targets Supervisor/Team Lead/Director of Operations/Executive Director.
- Missing-documentation alerts (Q16): missing Shift Checklist, incomplete Daily Log, missing Case Note, outstanding MAR, missing Incident Report, incomplete Sharp/Medication count — targets Team Lead/Supervisor → Program Manager → Director of Operations/Executive Director "for significant or repeated issues" (another severity-tiering signal).
- 16-hour automated critical-event detection (Q16, also M's own named module): scans Daily Logs, Case Notes, Behaviour Trackers, Contact Notes for keyword indicators across 7 negative-event categories and 5 positive-event categories (full lists in D Q16 — abuse/neglect, medical, behavioural/MH emergencies, medication events, missing-person/AWOL, safety/environmental, legal/external-agency involvement; positive: major achievements, behavioural progress, health/wellness, family milestones, program-transition milestones); if no matching Incident Report/Noteworthy Update exists, generates alert to the same 4-role chain. Entity-model relevance: an Alert of this type references a **source Document** (the flagged entry) and an **expected-but-missing Document type**, not just a Client.
- Data-breach alert (Q19): "incident is immediately reported through the incident reporting process... appropriate leadership notified: Team Lead/Supervisor, Program Manager, Director of Operations, Executive Director." **Distinct concept from Disclosure** (below) — this is an alert about an unauthorized/accidental exposure, not a record of an authorized release. Don't conflate the two entities.
- Scheduling/attendance alerts (Q25): staff failing to clock in/out, unapproved timesheet changes, scheduling conflicts, missing shift documentation, excessive overtime.
- **Observation on Alert lifecycle overall**: across every instance above, the only states the source material actually describes are "generated" → "sent to [roles]" → "reviewed" → "follow-up action taken." No document anywhere defines an explicit Alert status enum (e.g., open/acknowledged/resolved/dismissed) beyond the negative statement that certain alerts can't be dismissed by staff. This looks like a genuine gap for item 7 to flag as its own question, not something item 4 should invent a resolution for.

## Disclosure

- Q20: "Information should only be shared with Indigenous governing bodies, Nations, or community organizations when there is appropriate authorization or consent... system should support: documented consent records, information-sharing agreements, **tracking of disclosures**, identification of the individual/organization receiving information, date/purpose/scope of information shared." — **PROPOSED**, and the clearest direct spec for a Disclosure entity's shape: {recipient, date, purpose, scope, consent/authorization reference}.
- Q22: client/guardian records-request flow — "Requests for documentation should be reviewed and approved by authorized leadership (Program Manager, Director of Operations, Executive Director)... system should maintain a record of: who requested, date of request, information provided, person who approved, method of delivery." — **PROPOSED**. Structurally the same shape as the Q20 disclosure record (requester/recipient, approver, date, scope, method) — worth the entity model treating both as the same **Disclosure** entity type distinguished by a "recipient category" or "reason" attribute, rather than two separate entities. Flagging the pattern; not deciding it — that's item 4's synthesis call.
- Q22: "file access logs, sign-out records" — **this is access-logging (who viewed a record), not Disclosure (who received a copy/release outside normal access).** Explicitly flagging so the entity model doesn't conflate "audit trail of views" with "Disclosure log of releases" — they're different entities serving different purposes (one is the generic audit trail every document already needs per Q7/Additional Req §5; the other is a deliberate release-of-information record).
