How many staff total? How many per program/site?
There is no predetermined staff limit per program or site. Staffing levels are based on client needs, licensing requirements, contractual obligations, and appropriate staff-to-client ratios to ensure safe and effective service delivery.

Yes. NextGen Support Services may employ full-time, part-time, casual, relief, contract staff, practicum students, and approved consultants or contractors, depending on operational needs. Answer / Notes:
Yes. NextGen Support Services may grant portal access to approved external users, including foster caregivers, kinship caregivers, contracted service providers, and partner agencies, where access is necessary to support client care and service delivery.

Walk us through a typical shift for a front-line support worker. What forms do they fill out, and in what order?
Typical Shift Workflow for a Front-Line Support Worker
A front-line support worker is defined as any staff member who works directly one-on-one with clients and without any supervisory role. This includes all positions reporting below the Team Lead level, such as Child and Youth Care Workers, Support Workers, and other direct service roles. Front-line staff are responsible for providing daily client support, documenting services provided, monitoring client well-being, and reporting any concerns or incidents through approved documentation processes.
Start of Shift
At the beginning of each shift, the front-line support worker:
Reviews Shift Information
Reviews the shift checklist to confirm assigned responsibilities, scheduled activities, appointments, and any required follow-up tasks.
Reviews relevant client information, including current goals, support needs, and risk considerations.
Front-line staff have view-only access to:
Individual Needs Assessment
Healing Plan
Intake Screening Tool
These documents provide background information and guidance but cannot be edited, updated, or created by front-line staff.
Completes Shift Checklist
Confirms attendance, assigned client responsibilities, required tasks, and any required preparation before engaging with clients.

During the Shift
Throughout the shift, front-line support workers provide direct care and complete documentation based on client interactions, activities, and events.
Client Interaction Documentation
Depending on the nature of the interaction, staff complete the following forms:
Daily Log
Used to record routine daily activities, client participation, general observations, appointments, and significant events throughout the shift.
Individual Contact Note
Completed when documenting a specific one-on-one interaction with a client, including conversations, interventions, support provided, and client responses.
Case Note
Used for more detailed documentation related to client progress, concerns, behavioural changes, significant discussions, and service delivery.
Noteworthy Report
Used to document important observations, achievements, concerns, or events that require awareness but may not meet the threshold of an incident report.

Health, Safety, and Incident Documentation
Front-line workers are responsible for documenting health, safety, and risk-related information when applicable.
Incident Report
Completed when a critical or reportable incident occurs, including injuries, significant behavioural events, safety concerns, missing person events, or other situations requiring formal review.
Emergency Preparedness Documentation
Completed or updated when staff participate in emergency response activities, preparedness checks, or emergency-related procedures.
Behaviour Tracker
Used to monitor and document behavioural patterns, triggers, interventions used, and client responses to support behaviour planning.

Medication and Household Responsibilities
Where applicable, front-line staff complete:
Medication Administration Record (MAR)
Completed each time medication is administered, following medication administration policies and procedures.
Grocery List
Used to document household food and supply needs identified during the shift.
Monthly Sharp Count Checklist
Completed as scheduled to monitor and document the accountability and safety checks of restricted items.

Client Activities and Engagement
Monthly Activity Report
Used to document client participation in planned activities, community engagement, skill development activities, and recreational opportunities.

End of Shift
Before ending their shift, the front-line support worker:
Reviews completed documentation for accuracy and completeness.
Ensures all required forms have been submitted.
Communicates important updates, concerns, or follow-up needs to the incoming staff member and/or Team Lead.
Completes any outstanding shift checklist requirements.
Ensures documentation requiring Team Lead review has been flagged appropriately.

What does a supervisor or coordinator do with those completed forms — review them, sign off, file them?
Supervisor/Coordinator Workflow for Reviewing Front-Line Documentation
Supervisors and Coordinators are responsible for ensuring that documentation completed by front-line support workers is accurate, complete, timely, and aligned with agency policies, client care plans, and regulatory requirements.
1. Documentation Review
After front-line staff submit completed forms, the Supervisor/Coordinator reviews documentation for:
Accuracy and completeness of information.
Appropriate use of documentation types.
Alignment between recorded services and the client’s support needs.
Identification of changes in client behaviour, risks, progress, or emerging concerns.
Compliance with documentation standards and confidentiality requirements.
Documents reviewed may include:
Daily Logs
Case Notes
Individual Contact Notes
Noteworthy Reports
Incident Reports
Emergency Preparedness Documentation
Behaviour Trackers
Shift Checklists
Medication Administration Records (MAR)
Monthly Activity Reports
Grocery Lists
Monthly Sharp Count Checklists

2. Verification and Follow-Up
The Supervisor/Coordinator determines whether additional action is required.
This may include:
Requesting clarification or additional information from the staff member who completed the documentation.
Following up with staff regarding missing, incomplete, or inconsistent entries.
Identifying trends requiring additional supports, training, or updates to client strategies.
Escalating concerns to the Director of Programs or appropriate leadership team.

3. Approval and Sign-Off
Depending on the type of documentation, the Supervisor/Coordinator may:
Review and electronically sign off on completed documentation.
Approve documentation to confirm it has been reviewed and meets agency standards.
Add supervisory notes or comments where necessary.
Verify that critical documents, such as Incident Reports and Medication Administration Records, have been completed according to policy.
Certain documents may require additional review or approval from senior leadership depending on severity or organizational requirements.

4. Client Record Management and Filing
Once reviewed and approved, the Supervisor/Coordinator ensures documentation is:
Properly filed within the client record system.
Organized under the correct client profile and documentation category.
Retained according to agency retention policies and applicable privacy legislation.
Available for audits, quality assurance reviews, funding requirements, and care coordination when required.

5. Supervisory Access to Client Planning Documents
Supervisors/Coordinators have expanded access compared to front-line staff and may review, update, and manage:
Individual Needs Assessments
Healing Plans
Intake Screening Tools
Client goals and support strategies
Risk assessments and service planning documents
They ensure that front-line documentation reflects and supports the client’s approved care plan.

Who fills out the clinical forms (Healing Plan, Needs Assessment)? Is that always a specific role or anyone?
Responsibility for Clinical and Client Planning Documents
Clinical and client planning documents, including the Healing Plan, Individual Needs Assessment, and Intake Screening Tool, are completed by designated supervisory or clinical roles with the appropriate training, experience, and authority to assess client needs and develop service plans.
Front-line support workers do not create, edit, or update these documents. They have view-only access to ensure they understand client needs, goals, risk factors, and support strategies while providing direct care.
Roles Responsible for Completing Clinical Documents
1. Intake Screening Tool (To be completed during intake or 3 days after intake)
Primary responsibility: Intake Coordinator, Program Manager, or designated Supervisor
The designated intake role completes the Intake Screening Tool during the referral and admission process. This includes:
Gathering information from the referral source, client, family, guardian, or other involved parties.
Documenting client history, presenting concerns, strengths, risks, and service needs.
Determining program suitability and required supports.
Ensuring all required admission information is captured before services begin.

2. Individual Needs Assessment (To be completed 7 days after intake)
Primary responsibility: Program Manager, or Team Lead under supervisory direction
The Individual Needs Assessment is completed after intake and provides a detailed understanding of the client’s:
Developmental, emotional, social, and behavioural needs.
Strengths and areas requiring support.
Communication preferences.
Safety considerations and risk factors.
Daily living and independence goals.
Family, cultural, and community support needs.
The assigned role is responsible for ensuring the assessment remains current and is updated when significant changes occur.

3. Healing Plan (To be completed 7 days after intake and every 3 months thereafter)
Primary responsibility: Program Manager, or designated Supervisor
The Healing Plan is developed based on the client’s assessment information and outlines:
Client goals and desired outcomes.
Trauma-informed support strategies.
Therapeutic and relational approaches.
Behavioural support approaches.
Coping strategies and emotional regulation supports.
Roles and responsibilities of the care team.
Progress indicators and review timelines.
Front-line staff contribute information through daily observations, case notes, behaviour tracking, and individual contact notes; however, they do not independently modify the Healing Plan.

Role of Front-Line Staff in Clinical Documentation
Front-line staff support the clinical planning process by:
Reviewing client plans before providing services.
Implementing strategies outlined in the Healing Plan.
Documenting observations and client progress.
Reporting changes in client needs, behaviours, or risks.
Providing feedback during plan reviews.
Their documentation helps supervisors and clinical staff determine whether updates to assessments or plans are required.

Who is responsible for the Incident Report when something happens — the worker on shift, the supervisor, or both?
Responsibility for Incident Reports
Incident Reports are a shared responsibility between the front-line support worker involved in the incident and the Supervisor/Team Lead responsible for oversight. Each role has a specific responsibility to ensure incidents are documented accurately, reviewed appropriately, and addressed according to agency policies.
1. Front-Line Support Worker Responsibilities
Primary responsibility: Immediate documentation and factual reporting
The front-line support worker who witnesses, responds to, or is directly involved in an incident is responsible for:
Ensuring immediate safety of the client, themselves, and others.
Taking appropriate emergency action when required.
Notifying the Team Lead/Supervisor as soon as possible.
Completing the Incident Report as soon as possible after the incident occurs.
Documenting factual information, including:
Date, time, and location of the incident.
Individuals involved.
Description of what occurred.
Immediate actions taken.
Client response and outcome.
Any witnesses present.
Notifications made (family, emergency services, leadership, etc.).
Front-line staff should document what they observed and what actions they took without making assumptions, assigning blame, or providing clinical conclusions.

2. Team Lead/Supervisor Responsibilities
Primary responsibility: Review, follow-up, and quality assurance
The Team Lead/Supervisor is responsible for:
Reviewing the completed Incident Report for accuracy, completeness, and compliance.
Requesting clarification or additional details from the staff member if required.
Adding supervisory notes and documenting follow-up actions.
Assessing whether additional supports, interventions, or safety measures are needed.
Ensuring required notifications and reporting processes are completed.
Escalating serious incidents to the Program Manager/Director of Programs when required.
Monitoring trends and identifying opportunities for training, prevention, or changes to client support strategies.
The Supervisor does not replace the front-line worker’s responsibility to complete the initial report but ensures the report meets organizational standards.

3. Program Manager/Director Responsibilities
For significant or critical incidents, the Program Manager or Director of Programs may be responsible for:
Reviewing escalated incident reports.
Confirming regulatory or funding body reporting requirements are met.
Approving corrective actions or follow-up plans.
Reviewing trends across programs and implementing quality improvement initiatives.

Should support workers see full client files, or only the clients assigned to their shift?
Front-Line Support Worker Access to Client Files
Front-line support workers should only have access to client files for clients they are assigned to support during their scheduled shift or within their designated program assignment. Access should follow the principle of least privilege, meaning staff receive only the information necessary to perform their job responsibilities.
Client File Access Based on Assignment
Front-line support workers may access:
Clients assigned to their current shift.
Clients within their assigned home, program, or service area.
Clients they are authorized to support as part of their regular duties.
They should not have access to:
Unassigned clients.
Clients from other programs or locations unless specifically authorized.
Archived client records unrelated to their current responsibilities.

Information Front-Line Staff Can Access
For assigned clients, front-line staff may have access to:
View-Only Access:
Intake Screening Tool

Supervisors and Coordinators have broader access to:
All clients within their assigned program or service area.
Client assessments and planning documents.
Documentation submitted by front-line staff.
Incident reports and quality assurance records.
Historical records required for supervision and case management.
Their role includes reviewing documentation, monitoring client progress, and ensuring continuity of care.

Exceptions and Temporary Access
Additional access may be granted when required, including:

Emergency situations.

Leadership-approved operational requirements.
All temporary access should be documented and removed once no longer required.

Can all staff see medication records and incident reports, or is that restricted to senior staff?
Access to Medication Records and Incident Reports
Medication records and incident reports contain sensitive health, safety, and risk-related information. Access should be restricted based on role responsibility and client assignment, ensuring staff have the information required to provide safe care while maintaining confidentiality.

Medication Administration Records (MAR)
Front-Line Support Workers
Front-line staff who are authorized and responsible for medication administration may:
Access MARs for clients assigned to their shift or program.
Complete medication administration entries when medication is provided.
Document medication refusals, missed doses, errors, or concerns.
Review relevant medication instructions required for safe service delivery.
Front-line staff who are not trained or authorized to administer medication should have view-only access or no access, depending on their role.
Team Leads/Supervisors
Team Leads and Supervisors may:
Access MARs for all clients within their assigned program/site.
Review completion and accuracy of medication documentation.
Identify medication errors, missed documentation, or compliance concerns.
Provide follow-up coaching or corrective actions.
Program Managers/Directors
Program Managers and Directors may:
Access medication records for oversight, audits, quality assurance, and compliance monitoring.
Review medication-related incidents and trends.

Incident Reports
Front-Line Support Workers
Front-line workers may:
Create Incident Reports for incidents they witness, respond to, or are directly involved in.
View incident reports only for clients they are currently assigned to support when the information is necessary for service delivery.
Front-line workers should not have access to historical incident reports, incidents involving other staff, or incidents unrelated to their assigned clients.
Team Leads/Supervisors
Team Leads and Supervisors may:
Review all incident reports within their assigned program/site.
Add supervisory notes and follow-up actions.
Confirm required notifications and escalation processes are completed.
Monitor patterns and risks affecting client care.
Program Managers/Directors
Program Managers and Directors may:
Access all incident reports within their areas of responsibility.
Review serious or critical incidents.
Complete required organizational, regulatory, or funding-related follow-up.
Use incident data for quality improvement and risk management.

02 The Clients — How They Move Through Your System

The forms suggest a client journey from intake to discharge. But we’ll need to understand that journey in detail - every step, every decision point, every handoff.

Q5
Walk us through the complete lifecycle of a client — from first contact to discharge.
Why we're asking: We've mapped a rough sequence from your forms (Intake Screening → Client Info → Needs Assessment → Healing Plan → daily documentation → discharge), but we need you to confirm and fill the gaps.
→
What is the very first thing that happens when a new referral comes in? Who receives it and what do they do?
New Referral Intake Process
When a new referral is received, the Executive Director is the first point of contact responsible for receiving and initiating the referral process.
The Executive Director:
Receives the Referral
Receives the referral information from the referral source, which may include government agencies, community partners, families/guardians, or other service providers.
Ensures the referral is captured and recorded within the appropriate system.
Reviews Initial Information
Reviews the referral details to understand the client’s basic needs, presenting concerns, service requirements, and urgency.
Determines whether the referral aligns with NextGen Support Services’ programs, capacity, and service scope.
Assigns for Follow-Up
Directs the referral to the appropriate Program Manager, Intake Coordinator, or designated staff member for further assessment and intake processing.
Ensures responsible staff have the required information to proceed.
Initiates the Intake Process
Confirms that required intake steps are completed, including information gathering, screening, eligibility review, and documentation requirements.
The Executive Director maintains oversight of the referral process while ensuring referrals are appropriately assigned and moved forward through the intake workflow.

→
What are the steps between referral and a client being officially accepted into a program?
Referral to Program Acceptance Process
The process between receiving a referral and officially accepting a client into a NextGen Support Services program involves multiple roles to ensure the client’s needs align with available services, organizational capacity, staffing resources, and program requirements.
1. Referral Received
Responsible Role: Executive Director
Receives the initial referral from the referral source, which may include government agencies, community partners, families/guardians, or other service providers.
Ensures the referral information is recorded in the appropriate system.
Reviews the initial details, including client needs, requested services, urgency, and referral source.
Assigns the referral to the Director of Operations for operational review and coordination.

2. Referral Review and Initial Screening
Responsible Roles: Director of Operations / Program Manager / Intake Coordinator
The Director of Operations oversees the initial review process by:
Reviewing the referral information and confirming the appropriate program pathway.
Assessing operational considerations, including:
Program availability and capacity.
Staffing requirements.
Location/site availability.
Ability to safely meet the client’s support needs.
Resource requirements.
The Program Manager or Intake Coordinator:
Reviews the client’s presenting needs and service requirements.
Confirms whether the referral aligns with NextGen’s programs and service scope.
Identifies any immediate risks, safety concerns, or additional information required.
If additional information is required, the Intake Coordinator or designated staff contacts the referral source, client, family/guardian, or relevant professionals.

3. Intake Information Gathering
Responsible Roles: Intake Coordinator / Program Manager / Supervisor
The assigned intake staff member gathers detailed information from relevant parties, including:
Client.
Parent/guardian or caregiver.
Referral agency.
Previous service providers.
Other involved professionals.
The intake process includes:
Completing the Intake Screening Tool within 3 days of intake.
Documenting client history, strengths, presenting concerns, risks, preferences, and required supports.
Ensuring all required admission information is collected before acceptance.
The Director of Operations provides oversight to ensure intake processes are completed consistently and within organizational timelines.

4. Eligibility, Capacity, and Service Fit Assessment
Responsible Roles: Director of Operations / Program Manager / Supervisor
The Director of Operations oversees the operational suitability review, including:
Confirming program capacity.
Reviewing staffing requirements and available resources.
Ensuring the organization can safely provide the requested level of support.
Identifying operational risks or service limitations.
The Program Manager/Supervisor reviews:
Client needs and support requirements.
Program suitability.
Risk considerations.
Required accommodations.
Service expectations.
Together, the team determines whether NextGen can provide appropriate and effective support.

5. Acceptance Decision
Responsible Roles: Director of Operations with Executive Director Oversight
The Director of Operations coordinates the final acceptance review by:
Reviewing intake findings, operational considerations, and program recommendations.
Confirming readiness to provide services.
Making a recommendation regarding acceptance, service conditions, or alternative options.
The Executive Director provides final oversight and approval for program acceptance decisions.
If accepted:
A client profile is created in the system.
Required intake documentation is finalized.
The client is assigned to the appropriate program.
Service initiation planning begins.
If not accepted:

Additional System Requirements and Considerations
The following additional requirements should be incorporated into the overall portal design to ensure the system accurately reflects NextGen Support Services' operational structure, documentation requirements, and future scalability.

1. Program, Site, and House Management Structure
The system should support a flexible program and location structure that allows administrators to create and manage multiple service locations under each program.
The system should only include the following general programs:
Transportation
Respite Care
Group Care Program (also referred to as Youth Programs)
Family Reunification Support
Supported Independent Living (SIL)
Mental Health Services should not be listed as a standalone program. Instead, mental health support is considered a service component that may be provided within all programs depending on client needs and should be incorporated into relevant client plans, documentation, and service delivery records.

2. Program and House/Site Management
The system should allow administrators to create multiple houses or service locations under applicable programs.
For example:
Group Care Program (Youth Programs) may include multiple houses/sites such as:
Ravens Nest
Eagles Nest
Golden Bear
Whispering Harmony
Similar location structures should be available for other programs where applicable.
The system should allow an administrator to:
Add program-specific house/site names.
Edit or deactivate existing locations.
Assign clients to specific houses/sites.
Assign staff to specific programs and locations.
Schedule staff based on the specific program and house/site where services are delivered.

3. Program and House Information Integration
Program and house/site information should automatically flow throughout the system and be reflected in:
Client profiles.
Client assignments.
Staff scheduling.
Shift assignments.
Daily Logs.
Case Notes.
Individual Contact Notes.
Incident Reports.
Medication Records.
Behaviour Trackers.
Shift Checklists.
Monthly Activity Records.
Reports and dashboards.
For example:
If a youth is assigned to Ravens Nest under the Group Care Program, the location should automatically appear throughout the client's documentation, assigned staff schedules, and reporting records.
This ensures accurate tracking of:
Where services are delivered.
Which staff supported the client.
Which program requirements apply.
Which documentation belongs to each location.

4. Mental Health Services Integration
Mental health support should be available as a service component across all programs rather than as an independent program.
The system should allow mental health-related information, interventions, and supports to be documented within applicable client records, including:
Healing Plans.
Individual Needs Assessments.
Case Notes.
Behaviour Trackers.
Incident Reports.
Service plans.
Where a client's support plan includes mental health interventions, the system should allow this information to be connected to the client's assigned program and service location.

5. Document Locking and Record Integrity
All forms and documents within the portal should include a locking functionality to ensure documentation integrity, accountability, and compliance.
Once a document is completed and finalized:
The document should be locked to prevent unauthorized editing or alteration.
The system should record the individual who completed the document.
The system should record the date and time the document was completed and locked.
The system should maintain a complete audit trail of all document activities.
Locked documents should only be modified through a controlled unlocking and correction process.
Document Unlocking Authority
The ability to unlock locked documents should be restricted by default to the System Administrator(s) only.
The System Administrator will have the authority to:
Unlock documents when corrections or amendments are required.
Review and approve requests for document unlocking.
Maintain oversight of all unlocked records.
Ensure all changes remain fully traceable through the audit history.
However, the System Administrator may grant document-unlocking permissions to specific authorized leadership personnel where operationally necessary.
Examples of individuals who may be granted this permission include:
Director of Operations.
Program Manager(s).
Designated leadership personnel approved by the Executive Director.
Any delegated unlocking permission should be:
Role-based.
Clearly assigned within the system permissions.
Fully auditable.
Removable when no longer required.
When a locked document is unlocked:
The system should record who unlocked the document.
The reason for unlocking should be documented.
The date and time of the unlock action should be recorded.
All previous versions should remain preserved.
The corrected document should create a new version while maintaining the original record.
This locking and controlled unlocking process should apply to all documentation, including:
Daily Logs.
Case Notes.
Individual Contact Notes.
Incident Reports.
Medication Administration Records.
MAR Sheets.
Behaviour Trackers.
Shift Checklists.
Healing Plans.
Needs Assessments.
Activity Records.
Intake Documents.
This ensures that client records remain accurate, transparent, legally defensible, and protected from unauthorized changes while still allowing appropriate corrections when required.

For Client Incident Reports, the portal should include an attachment feature that allows staff to upload supporting evidence and official reporting documents.
The attachment function should support:
PDF documents.
Microsoft Word documents.
Images/photos.
The system should allow multiple attachments to be uploaded under one incident record.
Examples of attachments may include:
Government-approved incident report forms.
Photographic evidence.
Supporting documentation.
Witness statements.
Additional records related to the incident.
The system should maintain:
Attachment history.
Upload date and time.
User who uploaded the document.
Document type.
Access history.
Send notification to management about the submission
Audit trail.

7. Client, Staff, and Documentation Assignment Logic
The system should ensure program, location, client, and staff relationships remain connected throughout the platform.
When a client is assigned to a program and location:
Only authorized staff should access relevant client documentation.
Documentation should automatically associate with the correct program and location.
Scheduling should reflect the correct assignment.
Supervisors should be able to filter information by program, house/site, and client.

The system should allow authorized administrators to manage operational structures without requiring developer involvement.
Administrators should be able to:

Manage alerts and notifications.
