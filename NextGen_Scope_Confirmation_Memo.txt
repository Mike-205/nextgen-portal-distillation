NextGen Support Services
Staff Portal — Scope Confirmation Memo
Prepared: July 11, 2026

Purpose of this memo
When the original budget and timeline for the NextGen Support Services staff portal were agreed upon, the project was scoped as a documentation and forms portal. Since then, discovery has surfaced substantially more detail about how the organization operates — and NextGen has requested several additional modules that were not part of the original conversation. This memo lays out exactly what has changed, so we can agree on a revised scope, timeline, and budget before development begins.

1. What Was Originally Scoped
The original engagement was scoped as:
A staff-facing web portal to digitize existing paper/HTML forms (Daily Logs, Case Notes, Incident Reports, MAR, Behaviour Tracker, etc.)
Basic client record-keeping tied to each form
Role-based access for staff vs. supervisors
Replacing fragmented, duplicate, and incomplete forms with a single clean set

2. What Discovery Revealed
Two full rounds of discovery — form-by-form audit and a comprehensive non-technical discovery questionnaire — surfaced a much larger operational picture than the original form set suggested. NextGen is not just digitizing paperwork; they are building the operational backbone of a growing organization from a standing start (currently 0 active clients, scaling to a target of roughly 300 within 6–12 months, across multiple programs and yet-to-be-created sites).

3. New Modules Requested — Not in Original Scope
The following were explicitly requested by NextGen during discovery and were not part of the original scoped project:

Staff Scheduling & Shift Assignment
Full scheduling system — assign staff to programs/sites/clients/shifts, manage availability, handle shift changes and coverage requests, comparable to a platform like BrightHR.
Why it's new: Not part of a documentation portal. This is a workforce management system in its own right.

Time Clock, GPS Geofencing & Attendance Tracking
Clock in/out, GPS location stamps to verify staff are on-site, late/early/missed shift tracking, correction workflows.
Why it's new: Introduces location services, device permissions, and a new compliance/audit surface not previously discussed.

Timesheet Generation & Payroll Export
Auto-generated timesheets exportable to Excel, including regular/overtime hours, supervisor approvals, and payroll notes.
Why it's new: This is a payroll-adjacent feature; the original scope explicitly excluded payroll/HR.

In-House Communication Tool
A built-in messaging system (1:1, group, site-specific channels, announcements, shift handover messaging, push notifications) intended to fully replace the need for Teams/Slack/WhatsApp.
Why it's new: This is effectively a second product — a real-time messaging platform — layered inside the portal.

Automated Critical-Event Detection (16-Hour Documentation Review)
The system automatically scans Daily Logs, Case Notes, Behaviour Trackers, and Contact Notes for language indicating a critical event (injury, abuse concern, self-harm risk, medication error, missing person, etc.), and if no matching Incident Report exists, auto-generates an alert to leadership.
Why it's new: This requires text analysis / keyword-detection logic, not just structured form fields — a materially different technical build than a documentation form.

Client Billing & Funder Reporting Support
Capturing service hours, program utilization, and reimbursable expenses to support funder billing and reporting.
Why it's new: Introduces a financial/reporting layer previously not discussed.

Full Case Management System
Referral intake, eligibility review, acceptance workflow, program transitions, and discharge — as the organization's single source of truth for client records, since NextGen has no existing case management software today.
Why it's new: The original scope treated 'forms' as the deliverable; this reframes the portal as the agency's core operating system.

5. What This Means for Timeline and Budget
The modules above are not small additions — several are standalone systems in their own right (scheduling software, a messaging platform, a payroll export engine, and a document-scanning alert engine). Combined with a case management system built to scale to 300 clients across multiple sites from a zero starting point, the effort required is substantially larger than the original scope.

We recommend one of the following paths forward:
Phase the build: launch with core case management, documentation, and admin setup tools first (the modules essential to operating at all), and treat scheduling, time clock/payroll, communications, and automated detection as Phase 2 and 3 additions.
Re-scope and re-quote: revise the timeline and budget now to reflect the full platform as currently described, so expectations are aligned before development starts.
Confirm priority order: if budget is fixed, NextGen tells us which modules matter most for launch, and the rest move to a post-launch roadmap.

6. Requested Decision
Before we move into technical discovery and wireframing, we need NextGen to confirm:
Whether all modules above are must-haves for launch, or which can move to a later phase
An updated budget and timeline reflecting the confirmed scope
Sign-off on this memo as the current source of truth for what is being built
